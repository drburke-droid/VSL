How the board works & sources

Score

Each lens gets three ratings from 0 to 100 against the 27 lenses here. Oxygen & moisture: how close the lens comes to the most oxygen and the most water on the board. Infection safety: 100 for daily disposables, 50 for reusable lenses. Price: cost per day of wear, from 100 for the cheapest to 0 for the most expensive. The score is their weighted average, 40 / 40 / 20 by default; the faders change the weights.

Oxygen (Dk/t)

How easily oxygen passes through a −3.00 lens to the clear front of the eye. About 35 keeps the cornea supplied with the eyes open; 87 to 125 is the level researchers set for closed eyes, the toughest case.

Water

The share of the lens that is water. 50% or more is a “high water” lens in the standard classification. Silicone hydrogels carry oxygen through silicone, so they usually hold less water than older hydrogels yet pass far more oxygen.

Infection & inflammation risk

Infections are rare but can harm vision. Compared with daily disposables, reusable lenses worn during the day carry about 2× the risk of a serious infection, about 4× the risk of Acanthamoeba infection and about 10× the risk of an inflammatory reaction. No study separates 1-week, 2-week and monthly lenses. Sleeping in any lens raises the risk the most.

Keep in mind

Comfort also depends on fit, the lens surface, your tears and how you care for your lenses, and manufacturers don’t all measure the same way, so treat small differences as a tie. A contact lens prescription names a specific lens, so switching needs a fitting. See every lens on a chart ↗
