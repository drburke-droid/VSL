How it works and what it leaves out

Terrain. Elevation comes from the free Terrain Tiles dataset on AWS Open Data, which blends SRTM, Canada's CDEM, the US NED and other sources. Resolution is about 20–50 m within 10 km of you, about 100 m out to 60 km, and a few hundred metres beyond that.

The sweep. From the eye, each bearing is stepped outward along a great circle. A point counts as visible when its elevation angle is at least as steep as everything closer to you. The last visible point on a bearing is that bearing's sightline.

Curvature and refraction. Each point is lowered by d²/2R′, where R′ = R/(1−k) is the effective Earth radius. With k = 0.13, the standard value, light bends slightly downward and you see a little past the geometric horizon. Cold air near the ground bends it more.

Ground only. Trees and buildings are not in the data. For a tower or a high floor, enter your height above the ground.

Searching nearby. Up to eight of the highest spots in the search radius are tested at standing eye height (1.7 m), along with your own point at the height you entered. The winner gets the full sweep.

Seeing it. The result assumes clear air. Haze lowers contrast as e−3.912·d/V (Koschmieder), where V is the visual range and about 2% contrast is the threshold. The clearance figure is how far the target rises above the skyline just in front of it, compared with the 1′ of arc that 20/20 vision resolves.

Water. Sea-floor depths are flattened to sea level, which also flattens the few land areas below sea level.
